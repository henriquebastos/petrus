# Delivery

Delivery is the Ariad work area for formed intent becoming verified change.

Exploration asks what a signal is becoming. Delivery asks what the project is committing to change.

Delivery Work begins when the Navigator intentionally pulls work with enough shape to define a meaningful place in the roadmap taxonomy: Value / CV, Delivery Story, User Story, Technical Story, Task, or Maintenance. The work may come from a roadmap item, a clear request, a bug with known acceptance criteria, or a candidate promoted out of Exploration.

A clear request or known bug may instead belong to [Refinement](../refinement/index.md) when it cares for an existing capability without creating a roadmap-level promise. Delivery is the roadmap field. Refinement is the Workbench field.

This area is part of the core method. It defines how Ariad turns formed intent into bounded, validated, documented, coherent, and recorded change. The visual language can support that work, but the concepts and the visuals live in separate layers.

## What Delivery protects

Delivery protects bounded change.

A coding agent can turn intent into edits quickly. Without a delivery rhythm, the work can expand silently: a bug fix becomes a redesign, a refactor becomes a product decision, a documentation update becomes a new contract. Delivery keeps the change small enough to finish and coherent enough to trust.

## Delivery rhythm

Delivery uses a Delivery Story lifecycle: read and orient, plan, implement, test and validate, document, review, check coherence, and record history. When planning reveals that the work is too large for one behavior validation moment, the work becomes a Delivery Story and is expanded into smaller User Stories.

For non-trivial work, checkpoints pause the lifecycle so the Navigator can exercise judgment. Cadences name the boundary-triggered questions that appear when work changes state: whether to record history, show debt, collapse a parent, release, or pull the next story. The Driver should not ask permission for every edit, but it should stop where direction, validation, review, history, and transition questions need human confirmation.

## Language boundary

Ariad distinguishes **Delivery** from **Exploration** as methodological work areas.

A runtime may implement those areas as modes. Maestro may speak of delivery behavior or Builder Mode because it executes delivery inside Mirror Mind and renders its state through concrete surfaces. Ariad's method language should prefer Delivery and Delivery Work.

## Layer separation

Ariad separates three layers for this work:

```text
Ariad Delivery
  conceptual model, events, checkpoints, criteria, delivery lifecycle

Runtime delivery behavior
  execution of delivery inside a specific agentic runtime

Visual grammar
  rendering of delivery state, evidence, checkpoints, and closure
```

The visual grammar may use cards, blocks, checkpoint panels, flow maps, or other components. Those choices are renderings, not method contracts. A Delivery Story remains a Delivery Story whether it appears as a terminal panel, a web task, a commit proposal, or a spoken summary in conversation.

## Boundary with Exploration and Refinement

Delivery does not replace Exploration. It receives work when enough form has emerged.

A candidate from Exploration becomes Delivery Work only when the Navigator accepts the passage. Until then, it remains exploratory material: useful, alive, and visible, but not yet a commitment to change the product or project.

Delivery also does not absorb every requested change. When the requested change cares for an existing capability without becoming a roadmap-level promise, it belongs in Refinement as a Change Request inside a Refinement Story.

This boundary keeps the roadmap from becoming noisy and keeps delivery from pretending that uncertainty has already been resolved.
